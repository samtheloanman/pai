Product

PAI is a Mac-mini-sized home server. Models run on the box, memory lives on the box, and nothing leaves unless the owner turns on a channel that sends it.

  • First-boot interview. About 15 minutes. PAI writes a plain-English page of what it will and will not do, which the owner can edit. A short weekly check-in keeps it current.
  • Channels with honest privacy levels. PAI app (installable web app over the private network) at launch; Telegram and email at launch with stated caveats; Signal and Matrix planned for month 1; SIP voice later; WhatsApp and iMessage optional and unsupported.
  • Upgradeable. Starter ships with one memory stick and a free slot, then grows with more memory, a GPU dock, or a second box.
  • Safe updates. Signed A/B images designed to roll back if an update fails.
  • Open source. Everything we ship is MIT or Apache. PAI OS also runs on your own x86 mini PC.

Economics

Blocker: memory prices

DDR5 roughly doubled in 2026. Live Newegg prices on 31 Oct: 2 × 32 GB SO-DIMM from $775; 2 × 16 GB $550-620. The original sub-$1k prices all lose money at retail parts cost.

BuildMid BOM (parts, retail)Break-even price
32 GB box$1,133≈ $1,366
64 GB box$1,753≈ $2,078
Pro 128 GB (Strix Halo)$3,919≈ $4,568
  • Cheapest path found: factory-installed memory. A complete 32 GB / 1 TB mini PC retails around $829, so 100-unit quotes with RAM installed are the priority.
  • Fixed costs per unit: platform and payment fees about 8%, warranty reserve 5%, shipping and packaging, then a ~$100 profit target.
  • The business is software and support. Hardware margin is thin; PAI OS and PAI Care carry the model and ship first if hardware slips.
  • Early-bird pricing should be 28-33% off a real retail price, capped, matching what winning campaigns did.

Ground rules

  • Every public sentence follows the claims register. No medical claims.
  • No speed, watt or noise numbers until measured on our hardware.
  • Telegram is not end-to-end encrypted; say so. WhatsApp and iMessage are only "optional, unsupported".
  • Hardware prices stay unpublished until quotes are in.
  • Product images are labeled illustrations until there are real prototype photos.
  • Use fictional names in demos, never real client or staff names.
  • No vendor email, ad spend, deploy to production or Kickstarter submission without Sam's OK.
